# Balneological anchor assessment

Research date: 2026-09-17.

## Result

This audit found no independent semantic anchor for f84r.

The [Yale IIIF record](https://collections.library.yale.edu/manifests/2002046)
identifies the page as f84r, canvas 1006226. The [Yale detailed catalogue](https://pre1600ms.beinecke.library.yale.edu/docs/pre1600.ms408.htm)
places f75r–84v in Part III, called “Biological”. It describes small female
figures in fluids, tubes, and capsules. It also calls the drawings enigmatic.
This description does not identify a bath, spring, disease, treatment, or
language.

An AI review inspected the full Yale image for [f84r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006226/full/full/0/default.jpg)
and private inspection copies of nearby [f78v](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006215/full/full/0/default.jpg)
and [f81r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006220/full/full/0/default.jpg).
The pages show repeated visual elements: nude figures, green enclosed areas,
blue or green winding lines, and blocks of unidentified writing. This supports
an internal visual pattern. It does not establish that the areas are baths or
that the writing gives names or instructions. The reviewed sources do not identify
a caption relation for f84r. The text remains an undeciphered
script. This note uses “fluid scene” as a neutral description.

## What would count as an anchor

A useful external anchor must meet all four conditions:

1. A primary or specialist source must identify the external text and image.
2. The comparison must use several distinctive features, not only water and
   human figures.
3. A fixed caption, place name, treatment, or repeated formula must be tied to
   that scene in the external source.
4. The proposed relation must make a prediction for f84r or its neighbours
   before any VMS label is selected.

This standard prevents a familiar bath image from becoming a translation by
visual resemblance. It also prevents choosing an external word because it fits
an EVA transcription.

These comparanda do not provide a safe fixed label for f84r. Their value is
methodological. They show that a medical or balneological reading becomes
testable when a scene has a named place, a defined treatment, or a stable
caption relation. The visible resemblance of f84r to a pool is not enough.

## Source-order limit

The [Codex 4D record](https://codex4d.it/collezione/de-balneis/) reports altered leaf order in Angelica MS 1474.
It identifies a mismatch at 9v–10r: the De Prato text faces a Tripergula image.
Thus, present page adjacency can fail even in a known illustrated medical work.
Any later comparison must check the external manuscript's binding and text order.
The record prefers a 1258–1266 date and notes later dating proposals.
The broader thirteenth-century date is sufficient for this assessment.

## Named-witness image check

A later check used [Cod. Bodmer 135](https://www.e-codices.ch/de/description/fmb/cb-0135), another copy of *De balneis Puteolanis*.
The catalogue dates this copy to approximately 1350–1370, with uncertainty.
It records thirty-five numbered baths and illustrated rectos from f2 onward.
AI image review examined f2r, f3r, f24r, and f28r through the [IIIF manifest](https://www.e-codices.ch/metadata/iiif/fmb-cb-0135/manifest.json).
The primary agent also inspected f2r and f3r.
The review identified no fixed caption, place name, or scene sequence that connects this sample to f84r.
It does not exclude a broader relation to bathing imagery.
No text comparison followed. The anchor decision remains unchanged, and this image-comparison branch is stopped.

## Negative evidence and limits

The Yale catalogue's section label is a modern catalogue description. It does
not report a historical title for the scenes. Its older symbolic suggestions
are explicitly presented as suggestions, so they cannot serve as independent
anchors.

The nearby pages show recurrence. The reviewed sources do not identify a
stable sequence of named sites, diagnoses, procedures, or captions for them.
The page images also do not provide a second known-language text that could fix
one VMS word. Repeated VMS shapes remain shape-equality evidence only. They do
not become meanings through the bath comparanda above.

## Highest-information next observation

At full resolution, annotate every distinct fluid scene on f84r and mark the
nearest text block and line range with image coordinates. Repeat the same
annotation on f78v. Then test whether each scene has a stable one-to-one text
unit and whether the scene order repeats. This tests a proposed layout relation.
Even a stable relation would need an independently fixed label or formula before
it could support a reading. If the boundaries do not repeat, record
that this observation does not support a scene-to-text anchor. It does not by
itself refute a semantic reading.
