# Astronomical images and marginal writing

Date: 2026-09-29.
These source checks give no fixed correspondence between a Voynich word and a known meaning.
They also clarify the physical evidence for f116v.
The separate [Sun and Moon comparison](../../reports/SOLAR_LUNAR_NAMES.md) tests a restricted exact-name model.

## Planet names on f67r2

The author of the [planet-label proposal](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4089.html) compares a twelve-position pattern with an astrological chart.
The proposed alignment includes an approximate 160-degree rotation.
The author offers a provisional planet-to-label correspondence, with Jupiter at the top.
The examined discussion does not establish that this correspondence is unique or correct.

On [page 2](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-4089-2.html), the author treats the proposed correspondence as uncertain.
The search for similar sequences elsewhere follows that choice.
It cannot serve as a blind test of the initial correspondence.
The linked longer account was unavailable without registration. We did not examine it.

On [page 15](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-4089-15.html), different candidate dates give different planet names to the same label.
The examples also permit changes to the Moon angle and equinox position.
Thus, the examined examples do not select one name correspondence.
This does not reject an astronomical subject or every proposed alignment.

The exact historical chart cited in the proposal was not established in this review.
A separate library search exceeded its page limit. Its negative finding is excluded from the evidence.
No new Voynich name fit followed.
A future test needs a specified historical chart and fixed permitted alignments before examination of label matches.

## Three-sector diagram on f68v3

[Velinska's 2014 comparison](https://web.archive.org/web/20160410123428id_/http://ellievelinska.blogspot.com/2014/06/the-voynich-manuscript-geocentric-model.html) links to BnF Français 565, folio 23r.
The [BnF image](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b8451098g/f53.item) uses canvas `f53`.
The [catalogue](https://iiif.biblissima.fr/collections/manifest/4d402953e89fb48aa4313a55aace72bcb1b4c80d) places this folio at the start of Oresme's French *De caelo et mundo*.
Folio 1r belongs to a different work in the same volume.

The two upper sectors contain land-like scenes. The lower half contains waves.
Stars and a cloud-like band surround the central figure.
The sectors have no written captions or compass labels.
These observations come from the source image. They do not identify meanings for Voynich words.

The [Voynich image](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006197/full/2200,/0/default.jpg) has writing in its three sectors.
Similar geometry does not establish that these words name continents, directions, or elements.
The comparison supports a broad cosmological interpretation, but supplies no caption correspondence.
We did not transcribe or compare Voynich signs for this source check.

## Material evidence for f116v

The [McCrone report](https://library.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/voynich_analysis.pdf) is dated 2009-04-01.
The year in its current hosting path is not the analysis date.
Table I, on PDF page 8, lists sample 16 as black text ink from f116v.
It gives coordinates of 6.9 cm from the top and 2.9 cm from the left.
The report identifies iron-gall ink with a gum binder.

Table I states that no photograph was recorded for sample 16.
The report finds similar properties in the examined text and drawing inks.
It tentatively connects these results with production at the same time.
That source inference does not identify the hand, exact date, or writing layer of the selected external locus.
We did not identify which letter or word supplied sample 16.

[Davis's 2022 palaeography paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/keynote2.pdf), page 7, excludes f116v from its analysis of Voynichese hands.
This exclusion does not classify every word in the mixed-script entry.
Her [2024 imaging account](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/) discusses Germanic features and a possible connection with the writing on f17r.
Its date and meaning remain uncertain.
The images were captured in 2014 and released after processing in 2024.
Those dates are not manuscript dates.

Keep the f116v external locus outside the primary decoder.
The evidence supports an ink sample from this folio, but not a verified plaintext anchor.
No new reading or key follows from this review.
