# Child's later sign table

Date: 2026-09-30.

This review uses an archived copy of James R. Child's *Again, the Voynich Manuscript*.
The [14-page PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20090616205410if_/http://voynichmanuscript.net:80/voynichpaper.pdf) includes a proposed sign table and passage interpretations.
This adds method evidence beyond the [1976 prose review](child-method-source-review-2026-09-30.md).
It supplies no validated Voynich key or translation.

## Table and author qualifications

PDF page 14 has 21 data rows, including one unknown entry.
The table pairs manuscript shapes with transliteration labels and proposed sound values.
Transliteration means the representation of written signs with another set of symbols.
Some rules depend on position: `g` occurs initially, and `h` has different proposed functions in different positions.

The table also marks uncertain values. For example, it lists initial `n` as a possible reading for `m`.
Page 13 says that more examples are necessary to assess this possibility.
Page 12 gives different values for a shape according to the preceding sound and its position in the word.
These rules use position and neighboring sounds. They limit some value selections.

Uncertain sound values do not, by themselves, disprove a fixed map from signs to labels.
The unknown entry also does not establish failure across the whole manuscript.
This review assigns no equivalents in the Extensible Voynich Alphabet and measures no manuscript coverage.

## Proposed passage meanings

Pages 6–9 discuss folio 76r. Child gives a partial opening gloss and an interpretation of a later sequence.
He proposes a statement about differences in pronunciation, with an assumed reference to Goths.
Pages 7–8 explain the assumptions and qualify the interpretation.
Page 9 says that other content words lack obvious Germanic matches and that an English translation would be too speculative.

Page 12 states that some assigned letter values remain uncertain.
Page 13 expressly leaves exact word meanings unresolved.
These statements limit the proposed reading; they do not prove that a Germanic interpretation is false.
The reviewed material does not establish independently validated continuous text under the project's [validation protocol](validation-protocol.md).
