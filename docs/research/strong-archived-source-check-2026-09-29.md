# Strong's archived sources

Date: 2026-09-29.
This check obtained Strong's publication reprints, worksheets, and a typed correspondence collection from web archives.
The worksheets contain partial key material. This check did not establish a repeatable decoding procedure or validate a translation.
The earlier [source check](historical-and-middle-english-keys-2026-09-29.md#strong-source-access) recorded failed access through other routes.
Those failures remain part of the record.

## Source identity

An [archived index](https://web.archive.org/web/20070202230441id_/http://internet.cybermesa.com:80/~galethog/Voynich/) identifies a file of Strong's notes, publications, and worksheets.
The [99-page file](https://web.archive.org/web/20061215213410id_/http://internet.cybermesa.com:80/~galethog/Voynich/Strong.pdf) contains scanned pages.
Its first three pages reproduce the 1945 *Science* article.
Pages 4 and 6–12 contain the cover and text of the 1947 article.
The remaining pages contain typescript, handwritten notes, tables, and photographs.

The index identifies the collection. It does not authenticate each worksheet or establish its date.
The source manifest fixes the downloaded bytes with SHA-256 hashes.
The index also links keyboard maps for a transcription font. Those maps are not decipherment keys.

## The 1945 article

PDF page 1 says that wartime conditions prevent publication of the key details.
It describes arithmetic progressions and multiple alphabets, but gives no complete procedure.
Page 2 gives a proposed English passage from folio 78 and an author-name claim from folio 93.
It does not align the manuscript signs with the proposed output.
Page 3 claims consistent mathematical results and requests access to the complete manuscript.
These claims do not supply the missing conversion rules.

## Worksheet evidence

The file contains more than historical claims.
PDF page 75 has eight numbered rows, Latin-letter columns, and drawn signs.
Page 74 adds letter and number marks, arrows, circles, and corrections.
Page 82 places proposed words, drawn sign groups, and number strings in corresponding positions.
These are useful records for a possible reconstruction of Strong's method.

The following distinctions apply to the examined images.

| Material | Visible evidence | Unresolved conversion step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character table, page 75 | Eight numbered rows with letter headings and drawn signs | Which row applies, and how alternatives are selected |
| Number sequence, page 29 | Handwritten numbers and a small circular diagram | The operation, starting point, and reset rule |
| Proposed output, page 82 | Words, sign groups, and numbers in corresponding positions | The exact manuscript location and the complete selection rule |
| Letter tables, pages 90, 93–94 | Photographs of cards with numbered columns and letter groups | How these tables connect to the other sheets |

Pages 43–68 also contain counts, calculations, and graphs.
For example, page 43 shows tally columns. Page 66 compares distributions labeled with manuscript and reference-text names.
A frequency comparison does not itself specify a decoding operation.

The combined review found no complete instruction set that connects these components to a unique continuous reading.
This is a source-reading limit, not proof that reconstruction is impossible.
Some handwriting remains unclear. The review did not produce a checked transcription of every mark.

Any reconstruction must separate rules stated in the source from rules inferred by the researcher.
It must fix the sign inventory, table choice, number operations, start and reset rules, and treatment of unclear entries.
It must first reproduce a specified source example without changes selected for meaning.
Independent manuscript validation would remain necessary after that reproduction.

## The 1947 verification claim

The cover and article identify the authors as L. C. Strong and E. L. McCawley.
PDF page 6 describes the cipher in general terms and gives a proposed reading from folio 93.
PDF page 11 says that the decoding details will be available later.
The article's appendix gives historical discussion without a decoding procedure (PDF pages 10–11, printed pages 902–903).

The laboratory study reports no antibacterial effect from the tested material.
It then reports reduced sperm motility, with that effect absent after neutralization (PDF page 8, printed page 900).
These are reported observations about the selected material.
They do not establish that manuscript signs encode its name or the proposed use.
The study does not independently reproduce a manuscript reading.

## Correspondence

The [111-page correspondence file](https://web.archive.org/web/20160304100042id_/http://www.apprendre-en-ligne.net/crypto/bibliotheque/Voynich/strong_letters.pdf) contains typed text, not original letter images.
Pages 91–92, 95, 108, and 111 give historical cipher references or statements that Strong would withhold his method.
Page 108 suggests that repeated signs might form a passage-level unit.
It supplies no rule for finding or decoding that unit.

The page 95 editorial note says that the complete letter was missing and that its excerpt was transcribed in 1987.
The examined pages supply clues to Strong's work. They do not authenticate the correspondence or give a complete key.
