## Why

These records separate structural claims from plaintext recovery.
A reversed n-gram score can reflect boundaries and estimation rules. It does not establish physical reading direction by itself.
Repeated glyph strings constrain plaintext equality only under a declared context-independent or same-state decoder.
The released Antidotarium text mixes source languages and editorial material. It is not accepted as a clean Latin reference.

## Verification

The audit uses versioned author records, primary catalogues, and source-code inspection.
The directionality review confirms the reversal identities and the finite boundary counterexample.
Notebook metadata does not establish the requested historical version. The audit keeps that limit explicit.
The Antidotarium archive matched its declared size and checksum. No downloaded code was executed.
These records do not establish a manuscript key, language, or translation.

The image audit records Yale source hashes, target rectangles, and source metadata.
Two inspected labels are consistent with their transcription entries. The third remains uncertain.
Review found a clipped target crop. A larger source region now contains the complete selected run.
The primary agent inspected both revised target regions.
These are AI image inspections. They do not certify glyph boundaries or plaintext meaning.

The medical-image review found no semantic anchor in the compared sources.
The sources identify known medical image-text relations but no corresponding Voynich caption or treatment.
Review also found that altered leaf order can separate matching text and images in a known bath manuscript.
The result remains a bounded source comparison. It does not reject medical or semantic interpretations of f84r.

The layout record uses full Yale image dimensions, hashes, and approximate pixel boxes.
Root image review found four bounds that cut visible scene or writing edges.
The revised boxes include those edges and state larger boundary uncertainty.
The layout does not establish which writing region describes which scene.
These spatial annotations do not identify captions, reading order, or meanings.

The EVA note distinguishes connected strokes, historical analysis compounds, and linguistic units.
It records no universal rule that turns every repeated `e` or `i` run into one sign.
The strict extractor preserves accepted spelling; it rejects uppercase and bracketed candidates.
It does not recover connectivity information omitted by an upstream source.

Review of the mixed-cycle proof corrected the distinction between arbitrary and perfect matchings.
The final conditions distinguish feasible models, empty admissible sets, observed classes, and full inventory capacity.
With enough plaintext symbols and singleton classes, structural fit alone can provide no discrimination.

The mechanism proposal uses the earlier word-bigram failure to define a narrower prediction question.
A finite-window similarity gain would test local word context. It would not identify a historical copying process.
The existing manuscript groups remain exploratory evidence, even under a new split.
These three notes contain no new manuscript fit or measurement.
